    Reader setup

    Auto reads anything with A–F as hex. Force hex if your reader sends hex digits that happen to all be 0–9.
    Leave on auto until you see a wrong split, then lock it — or click one of the “Or, read as” chips on a read.
    The picker lists every HID device on the PC, not just readers. Yours is usually named for its maker — RFIDeas, HID Global, OMNIKEY. Names like “USB receiver” or “USB Input Device” are normally a wireless dongle or a plain keyboard; Chrome hides keyboard and mouse devices from WebHID, so picking one connects but never reads anything.

    Keyboard-wedge readers need no setup. If your reader types the card number into a text box in any other program, it works here — keep this window focused and tap a card. Nothing to click.

    WebHID / Web Serial are for readers that don’t type. They need Chrome or Edge, and the page must be served over https or localhost. A reader Windows has claimed as a keyboard or as a PC/SC smartcard reader can’t be opened this way — use keyboard mode for the former; the latter needs a native helper, no browser can reach it.

    Reads are held in memory only. Refreshing the page clears the list.
